The landscape of modern education is shifting rapidly towards digital automation. As institutions grow in complexity—offering more diverse courses, employing larger faculties, and managing limited classroom resources—the logistical burden on administration has reached a tipping point. Nowhere is this more evident than in the "{}\+Academic Timetabling Problem."{}

Traditionally, the creation of a department\textquotesingle{}s weekly schedule was a manual affair, often involving weeks of trial-\/and-\/error with paper charts or generic spreadsheets. A single oversight—such as scheduling two teachers for the same room, or forgetting a lab session requires three consecutive hours—would collapse the entire plan, forcing a complete restart.

The Intelligent Timetable Management System (ITMS) was born out of the need for a robust, intelligent, and user-\/friendly digital scheduler. This system is not just a calendar; it is a constraint-\/aware engine that understands the nuances of academic life, from teacher workload limits to the physical capacities of laboratory spaces.\hypertarget{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md6}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{1.\+2 THE CORE PROBLEM STATEMENT}{1.\+2 THE CORE PROBLEM STATEMENT}}\label{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md6}
The Academic Timetabling Problem is classified in computer science as an "{}\+NP-\/\+Hard"{} optimization challenge. The difficulty arises from the sheer number of overlapping constraints that must be satisfied simultaneously:\+


\begin{DoxyEnumerate}
\item THE TEACHER CONSTRAINT:\+ A teacher cannot be in two classrooms at once. Furthermore, every teacher has a "{}\+Workload Limit"{} (e.\+g., 20 hours per week) which must not be exceeded.
\item THE ROOM CONSTRAINT:\+ A physical space can host only one session at a time. The room must also match the requirements of the course (e.\+g., a "{}\+Practical"{} course needs a "{}\+Lab"{} room, while a "{}\+Theory"{} course needs a standard "{}\+Classroom"{}).
\item THE STUDENT/\+\+BATCH CONSTRAINT:\+ A particular student group or department batch cannot attend two subjects simultaneously. For practical sessions, the batch must have a dedicated block of time (usually 3 periods) without interruption.
\item THE DEPARTMENTAL CONCURRENCY:\+ Departments often have parallel batches. The system must ensure that while two batches might have labs at the same time, they must have separate teachers and separate rooms.
\item THE ACADEMIC PARITY:\+ Certain periods of the day are better suited for specific activities. Labs should ideally start in the morning or immediately after lunch, never late in the evening when students are fatigued.
\end{DoxyEnumerate}\hypertarget{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md7}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{1.\+3 PROPOSED SOLUTION:\+ THE INTELLIGENT APPROACH}{1.\+3 PROPOSED SOLUTION:\+ THE INTELLIGENT APPROACH}}\label{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md7}
ITMS solves these problems by moving away from "{}\+Manual Entry"{} and shifting towards "{}\+Algorithmic Generation."{} Instead of the administrator placing items one-\/by-\/one, they define the "{}\+Entities"{} (Teachers, Rooms, Courses) and "{}\+Allocations"{} (Who teaches what). The system then takes over.

The core of our solution is a Greedy Constraint-\/\+Satisfaction Algorithm. It operates by:\+
\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item Prioritizing the "{}\+Hardest-\/to-\/\+Schedule"{} items first (3-\/hour Labs).
\item Randomizing the search space to find diverse solutions.
\item Validating every single potential assignment against a real-\/time conflict matrix.
\item Providing "{}\+Smart Suggestions"{} during manual overrides, ensuring that even when a human makes a change, the system maintains integrity.
\end{DoxyItemize}

WHY FLASK? Unlike "{}\+Django"{} which is heavy and enforces many rules, Flask is a "{}\+Micro-\/framework."{} It allows us to build exactly what we need. For an academic project like this, Flask provides the perfect balance of speed, flexibility, and modern routing capabilities.

If the user moves the folder, the database moves with it. The Intelligent Timetable Management System (ITMS) is designed as a modular web application. The architecture is decoupled into distinct layers to ensure that the "{}\+Logic"{} (scheduling) is not mixed with the "{}\+Presentation"{} (HTML/\+\+CSS).

The overall flow of data follows this path:\+
\begin{DoxyEnumerate}
\item USER (via Browser) sends a request.
\item FLASK (Routing Engine) catches the URL and triggers a Controller function.
\item CONTROLLER interacts with the MODELS (SQLAlchemy) to query the DATABASE.
\item If SCHEDULING is requested, the SCHEDULER class is instantiated.
\item RESULTS are passed through the JINJA2 TEMPLATING ENGINE.
\item A dynamic HTML page is rendered and served back to the user.
\end{DoxyEnumerate}

In technical terms, our scheduler treats the timetable as a variable-\/set where each Day-\/\+Timeslot-\/\+Batch combination needs a Room and a Teacher.

The formula for the constraints is:\+ Satisfy(\+Session) IF:\+ Teacher(\+T) NOT BUSY at (Day, Slot) AND Room(\+R) NOT BUSY at (Day, Slot) AND Room.\+Type == Session.\+Type AND Dept(\+D).Parallel\+\_\+\+Sessions \texorpdfstring{$<$}{<} 2\hypertarget{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md42}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{5.\+2 PRIORITY SCHEDULING HEURISTICS}{5.\+2 PRIORITY SCHEDULING HEURISTICS}}\label{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md42}
The engine does not pick subjects at random. It follows a "{}\+Hardest-\/\+Task-\/\+First"{} heuristic. Practical sessions (Labs) are 3 hours long. It is much harder to find 3 consecutive free slots for a teacher and a room than to find a 1-\/hour slot.

Therefore, the algorithm:\+
\begin{DoxyEnumerate}
\item Filters out all Practical requirements.
\item Places them first across the 6 days.
\item Only then does it fill the remaining gaps with Theory requirements.
\end{DoxyEnumerate}\hypertarget{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md43}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{5.\+3 HANDLING PRACTICAL/\+\+LAB BLOCKS (3-\/Hour Rule)}{5.\+3 HANDLING PRACTICAL/\+\+LAB BLOCKS (3-\/Hour Rule)}}\label{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md43}
Our logic enforces "{}\+Standard Lab Slots."{} Labs are not allowed to start at random times. They must start at:\+
\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item Slot 2 (10:\+50 AM) -\/\texorpdfstring{$>$}{>} Ends at 1:\+20 PM
\item Slot 5 (02:\+00 PM) -\/\texorpdfstring{$>$}{>} Ends at 4:\+30 PM
\end{DoxyItemize}

This alignment prevents "{}\+Fragmented Gaps"{} in the timetable where a 1-\/hour gap stays empty because no class can fit in it.\hypertarget{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md44}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{5.\+4 ACTIVITY CLASS SLOT ALLOCATION LOGIC}{5.\+4 ACTIVITY CLASS SLOT ALLOCATION LOGIC}}\label{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md44}
Activity classes are automatically assigned to "{}\+Period 7"{} (the last slot). To ensure teacher continuity and workload fairness, the system checks who the teacher was in "{}\+Period 6"{} for that department and assigns them the activity class too. This minimizes "{}\+Teacher Travel Time"{} between buildings at the end of the day.\hypertarget{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md45}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{5.\+5 CONFLICT DETECTION ALGORITHM WALKTHROUGH}{5.\+5 CONFLICT DETECTION ALGORITHM WALKTHROUGH}}\label{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md45}

Academic scheduling is a subset of the "{}\+Resource Constrained Project  \+Scheduling Problem"{} (RCPSP). In our implementation, we define the following mathematical sets:\+


\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item S = Set of Subjects \{s1, s2, ..., sn\}
\item T = Set of Teachers \{t1, t2, ..., tm\}
\item R = Set of Rooms \{r1, r2, ..., rk\}
\item D = Set of Days \{d1, d2, ..., d6\}
\item P = Set of Periods \{p1, p2, ..., p7\}
\end{DoxyItemize}

The goal is to find a mapping function F:\+ S -\/\texorpdfstring{$>$}{>} (T, R, D, P) such that:\+


\begin{DoxyEnumerate}
\item UNIVERISTY CONSTRAINTS (Hard):\+ F(si).T != F(sj).T FOR ALL (D, P) if i != j (One teacher cannot be in two places at once)
\item PHYSICAL CONSTRAINTS (Hard):\+ F(si).R != F(sj).R FOR ALL (D, P) if i != j (One room cannot host two classes at once)
\item PEDAGOGICAL CONSTRAINTS (Soft):\+ IF F(si).Type == \textquotesingle{}Practical\textquotesingle{} THEN Duration(si) = 3 AND F(si).R.\+Type == \textquotesingle{}Lab\textquotesingle{}
\end{DoxyEnumerate}\hypertarget{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md139}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{13.\+2 THE HEURISTIC SEARCH SPACE}{13.\+2 THE HEURISTIC SEARCH SPACE}}\label{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md139}
The number of possible combinations for a medium-\/sized institution is astronomical:\+ (Days x Periods x Rooms x Teachers). A pure "{}\+Brute Force"{} approach would take years to compute.

ITMS uses a "{}\+Greedy Randomized Search."{}
\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item GREEDY:\+ It assigns the most difficult sessions (Labs) first.
\item RANDOMIZED:\+ When multiple slots are free, it picks one at random. This ensures that every time you click "{}\+Generate,"{} you get a slightly different but equally valid timetable.
\end{DoxyItemize}\hypertarget{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md140}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{13.\+3 COMPLEXITY ANALYSIS (Big-\/O Notation)}{13.\+3 COMPLEXITY ANALYSIS (Big-\/O Notation)}}\label{md__p_r_e_m_i_u_m___d_o_c_u_m_e_n_t_a_t_i_o_n_autotoc_md140}
The complexity of our core scheduling loop is:\+ O(\+S \texorpdfstring{$\ast$}{*} D \texorpdfstring{$\ast$}{*} P \texorpdfstring{$\ast$}{*} R) Where:\+
\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item S = Total sessions required.
\item D\texorpdfstring{$\ast$}{*}P = Total available time slots (usually 42).
\item R = Total rooms.
\end{DoxyItemize}

Even with 500 subjects and 20 rooms, the calculation remains well under the limits of modern CPU processing, completing in milliseconds.
